## Why this decision matters

A final review should challenge the strongest claim you are making. If you claim supported answers, inspect evidence support. If you claim reliable actions, repeat and interrupt them. If you claim useful time savings, measure the human review time as well as generation time. Define a rubric before reviewing the final output.

A peer review is useful because another person will make different assumptions. Give the reviewer setup instructions and an unfamiliar question, then observe where they struggle. Preserve critical feedback and the limitations that remain. Improvement is a traceable change and a rerun of relevant cases, not a rewritten success story.

## Vocabulary used in this session

### Peer critique

A reviewer tests the project against explicit criteria and unfamiliar inputs.

### Disposition

A finding is fixed, deferred or rejected with a recorded reason and owner.

### Release evidence

Observed behavior for the exact candidate revision and evaluation set.

review = [('supported',7),('correct_abstention',2),('unsupported_answer',1)]

critical = dict(review)['unsupported_answer']
assert total==10 and critical==1
report = {'dataset':'unseen-v1','candidate':'demo-r3','total':total,

print(report)

## Walk through the mechanism and its limits

The 9/10 superficially correct outcomes cannot erase the unsupported answer. Category counts preserve the nature of that failure. Candidate and dataset IDs make the result traceable. These are simulated reviewer labels, not actual measurements of your capstone; copy the report structure, not its scores. Once you tune on these cases, they are development evidence.

## Extend the example

Have a peer label one genuinely unfamiliar case and record disagreement before resolving it against policy text. Fix two findings, rerun the affected cases, and add fresh untouched cases for any new generalization claim.

The assertions demonstrate only the named local behavior. They do not execute your capstone, grade factual judgments, or prove that a real provider or external integration works.

## Build and challenge your own version

## Worked example

A reviewer finds that a correct answer cites an outdated policy revision. Classify it as a source-lifecycle failure, correct the active revision selection, and rerun all historical-date and current-date cases. Do not merely replace the screenshot with a successful query.

## Build it yourself

1. Freeze a candidate revision and run the held-out evaluation set.
2. Have a reviewer assess setup, support, abstention, ownership, and failure handling using a written rubric.
3. Select two material findings and implement or explicitly defer them.
4. Publish before-and-after results with sample size and remaining failures.

## Break it deliberately

The reviewer submits a case outside the original scope. Record whether it reveals a legitimate gap or a new requirement rather than silently expanding the claimed release.

## Acceptance checks

- Results include failure cases and denominators.
- Two review findings have a documented disposition.
- Claims in the README match the observed evidence.
