## Why this decision matters

A final review should challenge the strongest claim you are making. If you claim supported answers, inspect evidence support. If you claim reliable actions, repeat and interrupt them. If you claim useful time savings, measure the human review time as well as generation time. Define a rubric before reviewing the final output.

A peer review is useful because another person will make different assumptions. Give the reviewer setup instructions and an unfamiliar question, then observe where they struggle. Preserve critical feedback and the limitations that remain. Improvement is a traceable change and a rerun of relevant cases, not a rewritten success story.

## Vocabulary used in this session

### Peer critique

A reviewer tests the project against explicit criteria and unfamiliar inputs.

### Disposition

A finding is fixed, deferred or rejected with a recorded reason and owner.

### Release evidence

Observed behavior for the exact candidate revision and evaluation set.

## Learn each concept step by step

Work through these explanations before the completed example or exercise. Follow the inputs, mechanism and decision boundary rather than memorizing the definitions.

## Step 1 · Peer critique

Peer critique asks another reviewer to judge a specific output against the same source and rubric. Record the initial disagreement before resolving it; otherwise the final labels hide a source of uncertainty. A delayed independent second pass can serve when no peer is available, but should be labeled as such rather than invented collaboration.

## Step 2 · Disposition

A disposition records what happens to a finding: fix, defer with owner, reject with reason or narrow the claim. Connect the correction to a regression case and rerun it. Editing the report to remove a failure is not fixing the candidate. Fresh untouched cases are required for a new generalization claim after tuning.

## Step 3 · Release evidence

Release evidence binds results to exact candidate and dataset revisions and retains critical failures. Seven supported and two correct abstentions do not cancel one unsupported answer. State the blocking rule before review, preserve the counterexample and make a scoped decision. “Peer reviewed” without the actual judgments and source basis proves very little.

## Walk through the mechanism and its limits

The 9/10 superficially correct outcomes cannot erase the unsupported answer. Category counts preserve the nature of that failure. Candidate and dataset IDs make the result traceable. These are simulated reviewer labels, not actual measurements of your capstone; copy the report structure, not its scores. Once you tune on these cases, they are development evidence.

## Production evidence

A notebook or chat demo is an early prototype. Completion requires operational evidence: deployment/readiness, bounded work, rate limits, error recovery and persisted state. The 10% framing is a teaching heuristic, not a measured universal percentage.

## Apply the three pillars to A multi-user AI service deployed from containers

### Latency vs. Cost vs. Quality

Load-test offered vs. completed work, p95 and cost per accepted result at stated concurrency. Autoscaling settings are hypotheses until observed against downstream/GPU limits.

### Deterministic Evaluation

Test readiness against the actual database, restart/readback, isolated restore, denied owner access, overload rejection and a dependency outage on the deployed revision.

### Context Management

Budget resident model memory/KV cache and per-request context separately. Keep tenant/revision-aware application caches; never assume a process-local cache is shared across replicas.

## Failure and deployment work

Separate request concurrency, worker capacity and model/GPU capacity. Add admission control, shared quotas, bounded queues, maximum instances and a documented fallback/stop budget.

## Evaluation precision

Automate repeatable checks with fixed inputs and versioned expectations. Model judges, faithfulness scores and semantic reviews can be stochastic; record judge/configuration versions and disagreement rather than calling them deterministic truth.
