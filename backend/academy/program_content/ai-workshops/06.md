# Evaluate summaries for omissions and false commitments

## The engineering or business decision

Summary evaluation must examine omitted critical decisions and invented commitments, not just readability. A concise summary that drops a cancellation can be more harmful than a longer rough draft. Use a rubric with decision coverage, owner fidelity, date fidelity, and unsupported-action counts.

Compare against your annotated transcripts. Keep unresolved cases visible, and ask a reviewer to judge whether the confirmation workflow helps them correct mistakes. Measure review effort by the number of source lookups and corrections as well as elapsed time. Your final deliverable should show both a useful output and a known failure.

## Worked example

A meeting ends with “Do not publish until legal review is complete.” A polished summary lists publication as the next action but omits the dependency. Mark this as a material error even if every named participant and date is correct.

## Break it deliberately

Shorten the transcript by removing the final correction. Show how the output changes and why transcript completeness is part of the evidence boundary.

## Acceptance checks

- The rubric covers false commitments and missed dependencies.
- Corrections are evaluated against the transcript revision used.
- The final handover includes an ambiguous case.
