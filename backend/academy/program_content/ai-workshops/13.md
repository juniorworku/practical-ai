# Build a content brief and a factual boundary

## The engineering or business decision

Content generation is useful when it serves a reader’s task and stays within a factual boundary. Define audience, action, format, tone, source facts, and prohibited claims before drafting. A style instruction cannot authorize invented testimonials, performance statistics, or product capabilities.

This studio creates an informational product guide from a small fictional fact sheet. Separate verified facts from suggestions and creative language. Decide which statements require a citation or explicit reviewer approval. Build the brief so a different writer could produce a comparable result without guessing your intent.

## Worked example

A fact sheet says a fictional app exports CSV and stores drafts locally. The generated guide must not claim cloud synchronization, enterprise certification, or thousands of happy customers. A useful paragraph explains a supported workflow rather than inventing social proof.

## Build it yourself

1. Write a fact sheet with ten permitted claims, three unknowns, and five forbidden unsupported claims.
2. Create a content brief for a specific reader and action.
3. Draft an outline using only the provided facts.
4. Annotate each factual statement with its fact-sheet ID.

## Break it deliberately

A marketing request asks for “impressive numbers” that the fact sheet does not contain. The draft must omit or flag the request rather than fabricate metrics.

## Acceptance checks

- The brief names a real reader task.
- Factual statements map to supplied evidence.
- Unknown product behavior is not presented as available.
