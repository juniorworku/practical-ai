## Understand the decision: Measure the baseline and the decision value

## Before you begin

## Why this decision matters

A business case should count the work that remains after automation. Measure preparation, generation, correction, approval, and exception handling separately. Time saved is only one possible outcome: consistency, turnaround, accessibility, or traceability may matter more. State the denominator and uncertainty in any estimate.

Use a range rather than a single optimistic forecast. Do not assume every generated output is usable or every saved minute becomes cash. A pilot should test the most important uncertain assumption. Choose measures that you can observe in a small engagement and that the buyer agrees are relevant.

## Vocabulary used in this session

### Net time benefit

Baseline effort minus assisted processing, review, exception and other required work.

### Scenario range

Conservative, expected and optimistic outcomes based on explicit assumptions.

### Pilot success measure

An observable result agreed to test a material uncertainty.

## Learn each concept step by step

Work through these explanations before the completed example or exercise. Follow the inputs, mechanism and decision boundary rather than memorizing the definitions.

## Step 1 · Net time benefit

Net time benefit subtracts assisted preparation, routine review, incremental exception work and maintenance from the comparable manual workload. Keep task definitions and time units the same. The specimen’s 90 minutes is capacity released under assumptions; it is not cash income and does not guarantee those minutes can be redeployed.

## Step 2 · Scenario range

A scenario range changes uncertain drivers while keeping the model structure fixed. More review and exceptions can make benefit negative even when generation is fast. Distinguish routine review from extra exception time to avoid double counting. Show which assumption flips the sign before offering a fixed saving.

## Step 3 · Pilot success measure

A pilot success measure declares the acceptance rule and observation window before running. Measure all attempts and correction time on comparable inputs, with separate reviewed quality and support thresholds. A small synthetic sample teaches the method; it cannot justify a future client saving without representative workload and authorized observation.

## Baseline measurement and sensitivity analysis

### Specify the measurement contract
Define the unit, start event, stop event, sample selection, and exception policy before timing. For handouts, start when approved notes are available and stop when an instructor accepts a distributable draft. Include source lookup, correction, and rework. Measuring only model response time would omit most of the business workflow. Record individual cases rather than one remembered average.

### Calculate net benefit
Use an illustrative model: net monthly minutes = volume × (manual minutes − assisted preparation − assisted review − exception proportion × exception minutes) − monthly maintenance. The units must agree. If volume is 40, manual work 20, assisted preparation 4, review 10, exception proportion 0.2, exception work 15, and maintenance 30, the result is 40 × (20 − 4 − 10 − 3) − 30 = 90 minutes. It is not the 640 minutes implied by comparing manual work with generation alone.

### Distinguish capacity from cash
Ninety minutes returned to a salaried team is capacity, not automatically money saved. It may support faster response or additional work; it becomes a cash saving only under a separately justified staffing or expense change. Report time and financial assumptions separately. Do not multiply every saved minute by a billable rate and label the result profit.

### Stress the uncertain inputs
Run conservative, expected, and optimistic cases. Change review effort and exception rate first if those were unmeasured. With review at 14 minutes and exceptions at 30%, the same workflow can have negative benefit. That observation is useful: your pilot should measure review and exception cost before you promise scale. Use ranges to choose the next experiment, not to make a weak case look precise.

### Use paired comparisons carefully
Where possible, compare manual and assisted handling on comparable cases, with the same quality rubric. Avoid training the operator on every test case and then claiming general improvement. Keep some unseen cases for the final check. Report sample size, case mix, median time, range, and material failures. A small pilot guides a local decision; it does not establish universal productivity gains.

### Decision exercise
Build a table with ten synthetic cases, observed stages, error types, and acceptance outcomes. Calculate benefit after exceptions and maintenance. Identify the input at which benefit crosses zero. Write a stop rule such as “If more than two of ten drafts require reconstruction, investigate source quality before extending the pilot.” Explain the uncertainty that remains.

## Completed specimen: An editable benefit model with a stop decision

All inputs below are illustrative monthly assumptions.

| Item | Calculation | Minutes |
|---|---|---:|
| Manual workflow | 40 × (12 preparation + 8 review) | 800 |
| Assisted routine work | 40 × (4 preparation + 10 review) | 560 |
| Additional exception work | 40 × 0.20 × 15 | 120 |
| Maintenance | fixed monthly allowance | 30 |
| Net benefit | 800 − 560 − 120 − 30 | **90** |

Exception time here is **incremental** beyond routine review. If your measured exception duration already includes normal review, subtract the overlap rather than count it twice. Ninety minutes is capacity released, not automatically cash saved.

Conservative case: review 14 minutes and exception rate 0.30 gives 800 − 720 − 180 − 30 = **−130 minutes**. Optimistic case: review 8 minutes and exceptions 0.10 gives 800 − 480 − 60 − 30 = **230 minutes**. The range crosses zero; measure review and exceptions before committing. A simulated stop rule is nonpositive net benefit after the agreed observation window.

## Software-first practice

## Production evidence

A notebook or chat demo is an early prototype. Graduation builds toward operational evidence: deployment/readiness, bounded work, rate limits, error recovery and persisted state. Early checkpoints assess only boundaries already taught; future deployment controls are planned rather than claimed complete. The 10% framing is a teaching heuristic, not a measured universal percentage.

## Apply the three pillars to a discovery interview and opportunity scorecard

### Latency vs. Cost vs. Quality

Estimate response time, reviewer effort and cost of the current workflow; mark interview assumptions separately from observations. Compare at least two opportunities against a minimum accepted-output standard.

### Deterministic Evaluation

Turn interview claims into falsifiable workflow checks: input availability, reviewer authority and a repeated task. Reject an opportunity without usable evidence.

### Context Management

Inventory which approved source notes the workflow actually needs; exclude unrelated personal/client data and record source ownership.

## Failure and deployment work

Assign rate/budget caps, failure/manual fallback, hosting ownership, review duties, restore evidence and escalation before expanding the service.

## Evaluation precision

Automate repeatable checks with fixed inputs and versioned expectations. Model judges, faithfulness scores and semantic reviews can be stochastic; record judge/configuration versions and disagreement rather than calling them deterministic truth.
