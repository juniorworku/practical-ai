# Estimate effort, support, and pricing assumptions

## The engineering or business decision

An estimate should account for discovery, implementation, testing, review, deployment, handover, and support. Separate one-time work from recurring costs. Include uncertainty and the assumptions that could change the estimate. A price is a commercial decision; a cost model helps you understand what the engagement requires before making a promise.

Use fictional currency units in this exercise so the reasoning is independent of market rates. Model API usage from expected volume and observed calls, not only one successful response. Include retries, failed runs, review effort, and maintenance. Do not present a speculative return on investment as a guaranteed saving.

## Worked example

An illustrative pilot requires 8 discovery hours, 16 build hours, 10 testing hours, six review hours, and four handover hours. Adding a 20% contingency to 44 hours produces 52.8 planned hours. Provider and hosting charges are separate assumptions. A fixed scope still needs a policy for changes and recurring support.

## Build it yourself

1. Build an editable effort estimate by work package and uncertainty.
2. Use the downloadable economics lab to calculate net time benefit after review and exceptions.
3. Create three volume scenarios including provider retries and maintenance.
4. Explain how you would handle work outside scope and who owns ongoing service accounts.

## Break it deliberately

Double usage and increase review effort. Identify whether recurring cost or support capacity becomes the limiting factor.

## Acceptance checks

- One-time and recurring costs are separated.
- The estimate includes non-coding work and uncertainty.
- All numerical examples are labeled as illustrative assumptions.
