## Why this decision matters

The client needs evidence relevant to the business task, not only technical test counts. Report whether outputs are supported, reviewable, complete, and appropriately escalated. Explain sample size and which input types remain untested. A small pilot can establish feasibility for its scope without demonstrating reliability at arbitrary volume.

Use the agreed dataset and preserve raw results. Separate contract failures, factual errors, review burden, and infrastructure problems. If the same examples shaped every iteration, state that limitation and test a small fresh set before the final decision. Never replace a failed example without explaining why it was excluded.

## Vocabulary used in this session

### Client-readable evaluation

Results explained in terms of the agreed business task and limitations.

### Fresh review set

Representative examples not used to tune the current candidate.

### Conditional acceptance

Proceeding only with explicit unresolved conditions, owners and limits.

## Learn each concept step by step

Work through these explanations before the completed example or exercise. Follow the inputs, mechanism and decision boundary rather than memorizing the definitions.

## Step 1 · Client-readable evaluation

A client-readable evaluation explains inputs, rubric, candidate and outcomes without hiding behind one accuracy number. Sixteen complete cases, three clarifications and one unsupported statement describe different behaviors. Clarification can be correct yet impose review work. Preserve all twenty attempted cases and the strongest counterexample.

## Step 2 · Fresh review set

A fresh review set has not already guided prompt or implementation changes. Once a case is used to fix a defect it becomes regression evidence, not untouched evaluation. Retain its history and obtain genuinely new cases before claiming improved generalization. Record reviewer agreement and label uncertainties rather than inventing consensus.

## Step 3 · Conditional acceptance

Conditional acceptance states what can continue, under which controls and what remains blocking. One unsupported teaching claim may permit further reviewed pilot work but block unattended distribution. Separate format completeness, factual support, cost and correction effort. A favorable average cannot override a declared critical gate.

## Client-readable evaluation and decision reports

### Start with the decision
A useful report tells the buyer what was tested, what happened, and what decision the evidence supports. Put the scope and recommendation before tool names. A result such as “18 of 20 drafts preserved every required objective on dataset v2” is interpretable. “The AI scored 90%” lacks a denominator, a rubric, and consequences.

### Separate quality dimensions
For handouts, measure objective coverage, unsupported claims, source traceability, false acceptance, reviewer effort, and turnaround. A fluent output may still invent a requirement. An answer can cite an existing source ID while misrepresenting its text. Keep mechanical reference validation separate from factual entailment review.

### Classify results honestly
Report passed, failed, deferred, and not tested. Include raw counts and the most consequential failure. A small synthetic sample does not demonstrate performance on real client data, unseen languages, or scans. State those limits near the result, not only in an appendix. Label all fictional or role-play results as simulated.

### Explain the trade-off
Suppose version A accepts 9 of 10 drafts and includes one unsupported objective; version B accepts 7 and sends 3 to review with no unsupported accepted draft in the sample. The buyer must weigh review capacity against the cost of a wrong accepted output. Present both measures and recommend the next test, rather than declaring the version with a larger acceptance percentage the winner.

### Make the report reproducible
Include dataset revision, input selection rule, rubric, configuration revision, environment, and observed outputs. Preserve a small audit trail from claim to case record. Do not paste secret-bearing logs. A future maintainer should be able to rerun the evaluation and explain a changed score.

### Reporting exercise
Write a one-page decision memo and an evidence appendix. Include one accepted case, one rejection, one ambiguous case, a before/after comparison, and a recommendation with a condition that would reverse it. Ask a peer to identify what you did not test; revise any claim that overstates the evidence.

## Apply the three engineering pillars

## Software-first practice

Build interfaces, asynchronous services, API adapters and recoverable data flows before studying algorithm derivations. Use mathematics to explain a measured engineering decision.

## Production evidence

A notebook or chat demo is an early prototype. Graduation builds toward operational evidence: deployment/readiness, bounded work, rate limits, error recovery and persisted state. Early checkpoints assess only boundaries already taught; future deployment controls are planned rather than claimed complete. The 10% framing is a teaching heuristic, not a measured universal percentage.

## Apply the three pillars to a client acceptance report and change request

### Latency vs. Cost vs. Quality

Report per-accepted-output spend, p95 and reviewer corrections for the exact delivery candidate; keep failed and unavailable attempts visible.

### Deterministic Evaluation

Automate structural checks and attach authorized claim-level review. Block delivery when a mandatory gate fails even if averages improve.

### Context Management

Freeze source/prompt/model/context versions with the delivery manifest. Reevaluate after a change instead of silently expanding context.

## Failure and deployment work

Assign rate/budget caps, failure/manual fallback, hosting ownership, review duties, restore evidence and escalation before expanding the service.

## Evaluation precision

Automate repeatable checks with fixed inputs and versioned expectations. Model judges, faithfulness scores and semantic reviews can be stochastic; record judge/configuration versions and disagreement rather than calling them deterministic truth.
