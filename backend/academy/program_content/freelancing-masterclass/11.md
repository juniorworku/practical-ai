# Plan delivery milestones and manage risk

## Understand the decision: Plan delivery milestones and manage risk

## Before you begin

Bring your artifacts from sessions 1–10 of this program. Continue the same fictional training-provider engagement and keep assumption labels and revision IDs intact.

## Why this decision matters

A delivery plan should make dependencies and uncertainty visible early. Break work into milestones with an artifact, an acceptance check, a responsible person, and prerequisites. A milestone that only says “backend finished” tells the client little about usable progress. Prefer demonstrable slices such as “reviewed draft saved and reloaded.”

Maintain a risk register with likelihood assumptions, impact, mitigation, owner, and a trigger for action. The purpose is to decide what to do when conditions change, not to produce a long document nobody uses. Plan feedback windows and avoid scheduling all review at the end.

## Vocabulary used in this session

### Milestone

A reviewable artifact with acceptance, an owner and prerequisites.

### Dependency

Something another activity needs before its claim can be completed.

### Risk trigger

An observable condition that prompts a planned mitigation or escalation.

## Milestones, dependency risk, and delivery forecasting

### Make milestones observable
A milestone is a decision supported by an artifact: source readiness approved, vertical slice observed, evaluation reviewed, or handover rehearsed. “Backend complete” is difficult for a buyer to interpret. Connect each milestone to inputs, owner, acceptance evidence, and the next decision it unlocks.

### Plan around dependencies
Create a dependency graph for representative data, reviewer availability, access approval, implementation, evaluation, and acceptance. Work that depends on unknown source formats should not start with a promise of final quality. Schedule a short feasibility task first. Parallelize only work that can genuinely proceed without those answers.

### Maintain a risk register
For each risk record cause, possible event, consequence, likelihood evidence, mitigation, owner, trigger, and contingency. “AI may fail” is too vague. “Scanned notes may lose objective numbering; run a five-scan feasibility test before offering OCR support” gives you an action and a decision date. Track assumptions separately from problems already observed.

### Use ranges and update them
An estimate is a forecast based on current knowledge. Keep original and revised assumptions visible. When access is delayed, explain which milestone moves and what can still proceed. Do not silently reduce evaluation time to preserve a calendar date; that transfers risk into the deliverable without an explicit decision.

### Report progress through evidence
A useful status note contains completed evidence, current uncertainty, next decision, and help needed from the client. It should not be a list of hours spent. If the prototype works only on known good cases, say so. A blocker with a proposed smaller alternative is more actionable than a generic red status.

### Planning exercise
Build four milestones with owners and evidence, a dependency map, and five risks. Simulate a two-day delay in approved data and a reviewer absence. Reforecast the plan, identify a safe parallel task, and write a concise update that explains the impact without promising untested work.

## Completed specimen: A milestone and risk decision sheet

| Milestone | Artifact and acceptance | Dependency | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | labeled notes-v1 + agreed A1–A8 | template and instructor review | instructor |
| M2 | normal + timeout draft saved/reloaded | M1 fields stable | implementer |
| M3 | case-level evaluation + handover rehearsal | reviewer availability and M2 | operations lead |

| Risk | Trigger | Action | Consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reviewer unavailable | no slot before M3 | request new date or named alternate | acceptance pending, not passed |
| Real notes delayed | agreed intake date missed | continue synthetic implementation | real-data claims deferred |
| Source contradiction | two active objectives disagree | preserve conflict, request owner decision | draft review blocked |

A dependency delay may leave useful implementation work possible without making acceptance possible. Separate elapsed calendar time from effort. A risk owner must have an action they can take; “monitor closely” alone does not change the plan.

## Acceptance checks

- Every milestone produces a reviewable artifact.
- Dependency delays do not become false completion claims.
- The risk plan includes actions triggered by observed conditions.
