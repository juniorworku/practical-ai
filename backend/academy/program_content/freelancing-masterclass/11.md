# Plan delivery milestones and manage risk

## The engineering or business decision

A delivery plan should make dependencies and uncertainty visible early. Break work into milestones with an artifact, an acceptance check, a responsible person, and prerequisites. A milestone that only says “backend finished” tells the client little about usable progress. Prefer demonstrable slices such as “reviewed draft saved and reloaded.”

Maintain a risk register with likelihood assumptions, impact, mitigation, owner, and a trigger for action. The purpose is to decide what to do when conditions change, not to produce a long document nobody uses. Plan feedback windows and avoid scheduling all review at the end.

## Worked example

Milestone 1 validates synthetic data and the acceptance dataset. Milestone 2 demonstrates draft-and-review persistence. Milestone 3 passes the scoped evaluation and handover rehearsal. If source access is delayed, the plan continues with fixtures but cannot claim real-data validation.

## Build it yourself

1. Create three delivery milestones with observable acceptance.
2. Map client access, reviewer availability, and provider setup as dependencies.
3. Write five risks with concrete triggers and mitigation owners.
4. Simulate a delayed dependency and revise the plan without hiding what remains unverified.

## Break it deliberately

The client reviewer is unavailable during the final week. Show which work can proceed and which acceptance decisions must remain pending.

## Acceptance checks

- Every milestone produces a reviewable artifact.
- Dependency delays do not become false completion claims.
- The risk plan includes actions triggered by observed conditions.
