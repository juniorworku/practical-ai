# Manage change requests and commercial boundaries

## The engineering or business decision

Scope control protects both the client outcome and your ability to deliver it. A change request should describe the requested behavior, why it matters, alternatives, and impacts on data, tests, cost, and schedule. Small interface changes can have large implications if they introduce new roles or external actions.

In the simulated engagement, prepare changes locally for review rather than sending proposals or making commitments. Actual commercial and contractual decisions require agreement between the parties. The useful skill is making the trade-off concrete enough that the buyer can choose knowingly.

## Worked example

The client asks for automatic emailing of approved handouts. This adds recipient verification, send permission, duplicate-send prevention, audit history, and error recovery. It is a separate operational feature, not merely adding an email address field to the form.

## Build it yourself

1. Write three change requests: cosmetic, functional, and a new external action.
2. For each, identify affected acceptance tests, data flows, and operational duties.
3. Offer a smaller alternative for the highest-impact request.
4. Update the milestone and cost assumptions for one accepted simulated change.

## Break it deliberately

A “tiny change” allows assistants to approve drafts for instructors. Analyze the permission boundary before treating it as a label change.

## Acceptance checks

- The request includes operational and security impacts.
- Alternatives are tied to the buyer’s objective.
- Accepted scope changes update the evaluation plan.
