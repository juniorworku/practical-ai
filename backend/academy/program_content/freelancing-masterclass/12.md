## Why this decision matters

Scope control protects both the client outcome and your ability to deliver it. A change request should describe the requested behavior, why it matters, alternatives, and impacts on data, tests, cost, and schedule. Small interface changes can have large implications if they introduce new roles or external actions.

In the simulated engagement, prepare changes locally for review rather than sending proposals or making commitments. Actual commercial and contractual decisions require agreement between the parties. The useful skill is making the trade-off concrete enough that the buyer can choose knowingly.

## Vocabulary used in this session

### Scope impact

How a request changes behavior, data flows, tests, duties or schedule.

### Operational effect

A change in external state such as sending messages or publishing content.

### Alternative option

A smaller route to the buyer’s objective with explicitly different trade-offs.

## Learn each concept step by step

Work through these explanations before the completed example or exercise. Follow the inputs, mechanism and decision boundary rather than memorizing the definitions.

## Step 1 · Scope impact

Scope impact identifies the new input, output, user authority and acceptance promise behind a requested change. Automatic email is not just one extra button: it adds recipients, sending credentials and an irreversible external effect. Start from the current local-export boundary and enumerate what must change before estimating effort.

## Step 2 · Operational effect

Operational effect includes retries, uncertain results, duplicate prevention, monitoring and incident ownership. A timeout after remote acceptance cannot safely be retried under a new key. Exact recipient/content approval must survive dispatch and reconciliation. The exercise uses fake effects and plans; no message is sent.

## Step 3 · Alternative option

An alternative option lets the buyer choose a smaller workflow that still addresses the need. A local dispatch manifest for a human sender may avoid hosted sending while retaining review. Present consequences and unresolved estimates honestly. Accepting a change requires consistent scope, data-flow, milestone and test revisions rather than a verbal yes to a feature.

## Change control and commercial conversations

### Identify a change by behavior
A request is a change when it alters supported inputs, roles, effects, scale, quality criteria, operating duties, or agreed outputs. “Just send the handout automatically” introduces recipient selection, authorization, duplicate-send prevention, audit history, and recovery. Its size cannot be judged by the number of interface fields.

### Write an impact assessment
Record requested outcome, current boundary, proposed behavior, affected data, permissions, tests, dependencies, effort range, operating cost, and schedule impact. Offer at least one smaller alternative. For automated distribution, an export-ready draft with explicit manual send may meet the immediate need while avoiding a new external-action surface.

### Separate defect from changed expectation
If the app fails an agreed supported case, investigate a defect. If the buyer introduces a new format that was excluded, assess a change. If the original document was ambiguous, acknowledge and resolve the ambiguity rather than using scope as an excuse. Preserve the evidence and update the shared specification once a decision is made.

### Make the decision traceable
Use approved, rejected, deferred, or needs investigation. Record the authorized decision maker and the revision of scope, estimate, and acceptance affected by an approved change. Do not implement new consequential actions on informal enthusiasm alone. In this course, prepare local simulated decisions; no message or contract is sent.

### Protect the relationship through clarity
Explain the goal you share, the new work it entails, the alternatives, and the decision needed. Avoid defensive phrases or unexplained surcharges. The aim is for both sides to understand cost, time, and risk before work starts. A precise smaller option is often more useful than a binary yes or no.

## Completed specimen: A change request for automatic email

**CR-03, proposed only:** send approved handouts to participants. **Reason:** reduce manual distribution work. **Current scope:** local reviewed export; no external sends.

**New requirements:** client-owned sending account, approved recipient source, sender permission, exact content/recipient approval, durable idempotency key, send-outcome ledger, retry/reconciliation behavior, opt-in test environment and incident owner. A lost send response is uncertain; repeating with a new key can duplicate delivery.

**Acceptance additions:** stale approval blocked; unauthorized sender denied; duplicate submission produces one fake effect; timeout after fake acceptance reconciled; wrong recipient never silently repaired. **Data-flow impact:** recipient addresses and provider logs become new data destinations.

**Alternatives:** retain reviewed local export; or create a local dispatch manifest for a human sender. **Estimate:** investigate provider and test environment before quoting effort. **Decision:** defer CR-03 in the simulated pilot and finish A1–A8. No email is sent by this exercise.

## Production evidence

A notebook or chat demo is an early prototype. Completion requires operational evidence: deployment/readiness, bounded work, rate limits, error recovery and persisted state. The 10% framing is a teaching heuristic, not a measured universal percentage.

## Apply the three pillars to a client acceptance report and change request

### Latency vs. Cost vs. Quality

Report per-accepted-output spend, p95 and reviewer corrections for the exact delivery candidate; keep failed and unavailable attempts visible.

### Deterministic Evaluation

Automate structural checks and attach authorized claim-level review. Block delivery when a mandatory gate fails even if averages improve.

### Context Management

Freeze source/prompt/model/context versions with the delivery manifest. Reevaluate after a change instead of silently expanding context.

## Failure and deployment work

Assign rate/budget caps, failure/manual fallback, hosting ownership, review duties, restore evidence and escalation before expanding the service.

## Evaluation precision

Automate repeatable checks with fixed inputs and versioned expectations. Model judges, faithfulness scores and semantic reviews can be stochastic; record judge/configuration versions and disagreement rather than calling them deterministic truth.
