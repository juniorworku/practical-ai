# Deploy with client-owned access and a recovery path

## Understand the decision: Deploy with client-owned access and a recovery path

## Before you begin

## Why this decision matters

A deployment is a transfer of operational responsibility. Decide who owns hosting, domains, provider accounts, billing, backups, and incident response. Do not create a dependency on your private account unless the arrangement is explicit and appropriate. For the program, use a local isolated environment and simulate the ownership handoff.

A runbook must cover routine use and failure handling. Document health checks, configuration names, log locations, restore steps, and escalation. Keep secret values outside the document. Verify a backup restore into an isolated database. A deployment checklist is evidence of work only when its steps have actually been performed and recorded.

## Vocabulary used in this session

### Operational ownership

The party responsible for service accounts, data, recurring charges and incident response.

### Recovery evidence

An observed restart or restore result rather than an unchecked backup file.

### Credential separation

Client operation does not depend on an implementer’s private personal key.

## Learn each concept step by step

Work through these explanations before the completed example or exercise. Follow the inputs, mechanism and decision boundary rather than memorizing the definitions.

## Step 1 · Operational ownership

Operational ownership identifies who can administer hosting, data, billing, review and incidents after handover. The client’s service identity must be separate from the implementer’s temporary role. A named account in a table is not proof the client can operate it; require a fresh-operator action and actual readback.

## Step 2 · Recovery evidence

Recovery evidence ties restart and restore observations to a release, snapshot and representative records. Restoring into an isolated target protects the source during rehearsal. An intact backup file is not enough. Show which data point was recovered and which post-snapshot work would be absent; keep recovery limits explicit.

## Step 3 · Credential separation

Credential separation removes the implementer without destroying runtime or administrator access. List configuration names and the agreed secret-store procedure, never secret values in the runbook. A local role simulation teaches the access rule; it does not prove a real cloud IAM policy or production disaster-recovery plan.

## Operational ownership, rollout, and recovery

### Transfer capability, not just files
Handover succeeds when the client can run, administer, pay for, and recover the service without your private accounts. Create an ownership matrix for hosting, domain, provider account, database, backups, user administration, and incident decisions. Include a named fallback owner where possible.

### Rehearse the deployment path
Record configuration names, supported runtime, migration command, health check, and a simple smoke case. Keep secret values in the agreed secret store. Deploy into an isolated environment first and record the version actually running. “Works on my laptop” does not prove the client environment has its dependencies or network access.

### Roll out in stages
Use a limited group or shadow output before wider use. Choose promotion and rollback criteria in advance: quality, review burden, latency, error rate, and cost. Shadow generation must not create external effects. A rollback needs a known previous version and compatible data, not merely an old container image.

### Restore data as a test
A backup file is not evidence of recoverability. Restore into a separate database, verify a known record and access boundaries, and record time and errors. Understand which data would be lost between backups and how long restoration takes. Those observations inform recovery objectives; they do not guarantee every future incident will match the rehearsal.

### Remove implementer access
Use a simulated implementer role and revoke it after handover. Verify the client administrator and routine operator still work. Rotate shared development credentials where necessary. Check deployment automation does not depend on your personal token.

### Handover exercise
Create the ownership matrix, deploy the pilot locally, save a record, restart, restore into isolation, and remove implementer access. Have a peer follow the runbook without coaching. Record any undocumented assumption and repair the instructions before acceptance.

The runbook lists configuration **names**, never secret values. State how the administrator finds the agreed secret store and rotates access. Record exact release and backup IDs, observed restore content and limitations. A checked box without an observation is not a handover test.

For the local simulation, use distinct roles and synthetic records. Revoking an implementer role must not remove the application’s separate service identity or the client administrator’s recovery access.

## Apply the three engineering pillars

## Software-first practice

Build interfaces, asynchronous services, API adapters and recoverable data flows before studying algorithm derivations. Use mathematics to explain a measured engineering decision.

## Production evidence

A notebook or chat demo is an early prototype. Completion requires operational evidence: deployment/readiness, bounded work, rate limits, error recovery and persisted state. The 10% framing is a teaching heuristic, not a measured universal percentage.

## Apply the three pillars to an operator runbook and incident drill

### Latency vs. Cost vs. Quality

Include routine usage, alert response, fallback and restore labor in the ongoing service budget. Compare capacity assumptions with actual drill observations.

### Deterministic Evaluation

Have a fresh operator restart, read back, deny wrong-owner access and restore isolated synthetic records; attach transcripts and unresolved failures.

### Context Management

Define who can refresh data, revoke access, invalidate caches and prune logs. A handover needs lifecycle ownership, not a folder of prompts.

## Failure and deployment work

Assign rate/budget caps, failure/manual fallback, hosting ownership, review duties, restore evidence and escalation before expanding the service.

## Evaluation precision

Automate repeatable checks with fixed inputs and versioned expectations. Model judges, faithfulness scores and semantic reviews can be stochastic; record judge/configuration versions and disagreement rather than calling them deterministic truth.
