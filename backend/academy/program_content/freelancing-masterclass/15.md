## Understand the decision: Support boundaries, maintenance, and incident review

## Before you begin

## Why this decision matters

Support is a defined service, not an indefinite promise to fix everything immediately. Specify the supported environment, reporting channel, coverage assumptions, severity definitions, and responsibility for third-party changes. Distinguish response targets from resolution guarantees. For a simulated engagement, use illustrative assumptions rather than a commitment to real clients.

Plan maintenance around dependencies that change: model behavior, provider availability, source documents, and application versions. A model or prompt revision can change quality even when the UI code is unchanged. Rerun representative evaluations before promotion and keep a recovery path. Incident review should identify contributing conditions and corrective checks rather than assign blame.

## Vocabulary used in this session

### Support scope

Defined coverage, environment, responsibilities and escalation assumptions.

### Response target

A planned time to acknowledge or begin handling a report, distinct from guaranteed resolution.

### Incident review

A record of observations, recovery and contributing conditions with testable corrective actions.

## Learn each concept step by step

Work through these explanations before the completed example or exercise. Follow the inputs, mechanism and decision boundary rather than memorizing the definitions.

## Step 1 · Support scope

Support scope states covered versions, environments, hours and responsibilities. Separate operational troubleshooting from new features and factual approval duties. A service must say who can pause exports and who resolves source contradictions. Support capacity is recurring effort that belongs in the estimate, not an unlimited promise bundled with a pilot.

## Step 2 · Response target

A response target is time to acknowledge or triage under an agreed coverage assumption; it is not a guaranteed resolution time. Severity depends on impact and available fallback. Preserve uncertainty about a provider outage rather than promising a fix outside your control. Escalate to the named owner with evidence and a safe manual path.

## Step 3 · Incident review

An incident review reconstructs timeline, affected source/release IDs, contributing conditions, containment, recovery and prevention checks. Restarting a service cannot repair stale policy selection. Keep original drafts and supersession history, then require new exact-revision review. A blameless review still needs concrete owners and testable corrective actions.

## Support design and incident learning

### Specify the supported service
Name the environment, versions, features, reporting route, coverage window, and excluded changes. Distinguish response target from resolution guarantee. An incident caused by a third-party outage may be outside your repair control while still requiring diagnosis and a fallback. Use illustrative assumptions in this course rather than making real service commitments.

### Classify incidents by consequence
Define examples: unavailable drafting, unauthorized access, unsupported accepted content, slow response, and cosmetic defects. A low error count does not make an authorization failure low severity. Record affected users, exposure period, workaround, and the decision owner. Avoid including private content in broadly shared incident notes.

### Contain, recover, then investigate
Pause external effects or promotion when needed. Preserve enough evidence to understand the incident without copying secrets. Restore a safe operating path, including manual operation if appropriate. After recovery, reconstruct the timeline, contributing conditions, detection gap, and corrective actions.

### Treat model and source changes as releases
A prompt, model, retrieval setting, or policy revision can change behavior without a frontend code change. Keep their revisions and rerun representative evaluations before promotion. Maintain a rollback or source-deactivation path. A restart does not fix a stale policy index.

### Close actions with evidence
Assign each corrective action an owner, due condition, and verification case. “Be more careful” is not a control. “The index activation step rejects a source revision without a completed evaluation record” is testable. Review whether temporary controls should be retired after the underlying fix.

### Incident exercise
Simulate a source-policy update that makes an accepted handout stale. Identify affected outputs, disable the stale revision, prepare a local reviewer note, restore the correct source, and add a regression case. Write a short incident review with facts separated from hypotheses.

## Apply the three engineering pillars

## Software-first practice

Build interfaces, asynchronous services, API adapters and recoverable data flows before studying algorithm derivations. Use mathematics to explain a measured engineering decision.

## Production evidence

A notebook or chat demo is an early prototype. Graduation builds toward operational evidence: deployment/readiness, bounded work, rate limits, error recovery and persisted state. Early checkpoints assess only boundaries already taught; future deployment controls are planned rather than claimed complete. The 10% framing is a teaching heuristic, not a measured universal percentage.

## Apply the three pillars to an operator runbook and incident drill

### Latency vs. Cost vs. Quality

Include routine usage, alert response, fallback and restore labor in the ongoing service budget. Compare capacity assumptions with actual drill observations.

### Deterministic Evaluation

Have a fresh operator restart, read back, deny wrong-owner access and restore isolated synthetic records; attach transcripts and unresolved failures.

### Context Management

Define who can refresh data, revoke access, invalidate caches and prune logs. A handover needs lifecycle ownership, not a folder of prompts.

## Failure and deployment work

Assign rate/budget caps, failure/manual fallback, hosting ownership, review duties, restore evidence and escalation before expanding the service.

## Evaluation precision

Automate repeatable checks with fixed inputs and versioned expectations. Model judges, faithfulness scores and semantic reviews can be stochastic; record judge/configuration versions and disagreement rather than calling them deterministic truth.
