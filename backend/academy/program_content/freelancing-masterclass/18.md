# Complete the simulated client engagement

## The engineering or business decision

The final practicum assembles one coherent engagement. Discovery, offer, scope, estimate, prototype, evaluation, acceptance, and handover must agree with one another. A claim that appears in the proposal should either have supporting evidence in the final package or be explicitly revised. This cross-document consistency is a practical delivery skill.

Use a reviewer to challenge the package as a buyer, user, and future maintainer. Ask them to identify what they are receiving, what remains manual, what evidence supports quality, and who responds when it breaks. Your outcome is a reusable delivery process and a defensible portfolio, not a promise of income or immediate clients.

## Worked example

The final package for the fictional training provider contains a scoped draft-and-review app, a ten-case evaluation, a client-readable report, editable cost assumptions, a runbook, and an acceptance record. It excludes automated sending and documents that those actions were never tested or authorized.

## Build it yourself

1. Create an index linking every artifact from the six modules.
2. Audit promises in the offer against the final acceptance and evaluation records.
3. Run a complete handover rehearsal with a fresh reviewer.
4. Write a retrospective and a reusable template pack based on what worked and failed.

## Break it deliberately

The reviewer finds a mismatch between the promised supported format and the tested format. Fix the capability or narrow the claim, then rerun the affected checks.

## Acceptance checks

- Scope, implementation, evidence, and handover are consistent.
- The final package includes limitations and operational ownership.
- The retrospective produces specific improvements to the next engagement.

## Deliverable

Save **`client-06/final-delivery-index.md`** in your separate program workspace. Include your inputs, actual outputs, decisions, and unresolved limitations. Budget about **180 minutes** for reading, implementation and review; this is an estimate, not a timed requirement.
