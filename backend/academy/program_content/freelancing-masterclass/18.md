## Why this decision matters

The final practicum assembles one coherent engagement. Discovery, offer, scope, estimate, prototype, evaluation, acceptance, and handover must agree with one another. A claim that appears in the proposal should either have supporting evidence in the final package or be explicitly revised. This cross-document consistency is a practical delivery skill.

Use a reviewer to challenge the package as a buyer, user, and future maintainer. Ask them to identify what they are receiving, what remains manual, what evidence supports quality, and who responds when it breaks. Your outcome is a reusable delivery process and a defensible portfolio, not a promise of income or immediate clients.

## Vocabulary used in this session

### Delivery consistency

Offer, scope, implementation, acceptance and handover describe the same actual release.

### Retrospective

A review of what happened and concrete improvements for the next engagement.

### Reusable template

An adaptable structure based on tested delivery work rather than copied unexamined claims.

## Learn each concept step by step

Work through these explanations before the completed example or exercise. Follow the inputs, mechanism and decision boundary rather than memorizing the definitions.

## Step 1 · Delivery consistency

Delivery consistency means offer, implementation, acceptance and handover describe the same revision and capability. A text-only prototype cannot fulfill a PDF promise by changing one test label. Trace each promise to actual files and observations; implement/evaluate the missing capability or narrow the accepted scope explicitly.

## Step 2 · Retrospective

A retrospective separates what happened, why it mattered and what will change next time. Retain the failed first attempt and delayed dependencies instead of rewriting a success story. Attach a corrective check and owner to recurring problems. A completed template is useful only if it changes the next engagement’s decisions.

## Step 3 · Reusable template

A reusable template contains field definitions, evidence requirements and honest statuses, not prefilled success. A future project must replace assumptions, fixture IDs and actual observations. Keep pending work visible with owners and stop claiming completeness where evidence is missing. Mechanical consistency checking complements, rather than replaces, a fresh-operator handover rehearsal.

## Engagement synthesis and repeatable delivery

### Audit consistency across the package
Trace each promise in the service offer through scope, implementation, evaluation, acceptance, and handover. If the offer promises editable drafts, a screenshot of generated text is insufficient; demonstrate editing, saving, and reopening a revision. If a supported format was never tested, either test it or revise the promise before closing the engagement.

### Create a delivery index
List artifact, purpose, revision, owner, acceptance state, and location in the simulated project. Include the discovery dossier, baseline, offer, scope, estimate, data map, pilot, evaluation, change log, runbook, training guide, and acceptance record. A directory full of files is less useful than an index explaining how they support the delivery decision.

### Rehearse three perspectives
As a buyer, ask what outcome was demonstrated and what costs recur. As an operator, complete a normal task and handle a failure. As a maintainer, restore a record, change a configuration, and locate the relevant evaluation. Record where the handover depends on the implementer supplying undocumented knowledge.

### Keep unresolved work explicit
For each limitation, record consequence, owner, next evidence, and whether it blocks acceptance. A backlog entry does not automatically make a material failure acceptable. The authorized reviewer must make that decision. In the course, label that decision as simulated.

### Improve the process from observed friction
Choose one discovery question, one acceptance scenario, one technical control, and one handover instruction to improve based on the engagement. Explain the event that motivated each change. Keep reusable templates small enough to understand, and remove fields that no one used to make a decision.

### Final practicum
Run the complete source-to-approved-export journey on unseen synthetic cases, including one failure and recovery. Audit the evidence and present a defensible recommendation: expand, revise, pause, or stop. The program outcome is the ability to deliver and explain a bounded service responsibly, supported by your actual work.

# Complete simulated client engagement

The capstone is a coherent client delivery, not a claim that a real client signed off. Use Python 3.12; no third-party package, model account or another program is needed. The client/ files are complete synthetic artifacts, and engagement.py actually runs their pilot and recovery checks.

## Build the engagement in order

1. Read baseline.json: 40 items × (8−5) minutes − 30 maintenance minutes = 90 projected minutes/week. These are fictional assumptions; they are not observed ROI. Match the buyer and workflow to the discovery and baseline lessons 1–3.
2. Compare offer.json and scope.json from lessons 4–6. Every promise must be in scope and have an acceptance ID. Automatic publication, real hosting and factual client acceptance are explicitly excluded. A fixed estimate is not permission to expand scope.
3. Read data-policy.json and requests.csv from lessons 7–9. Only id, source and text are approved. policy-v1 permits a verbatim source draft. An empty source or unapproved revision routes to needs_source; no model fills the gap.
4. acceptance.json fixes expected outcomes before the pilot runs. engagement.py opens a fresh SQLite file, writes drafts, closes it and reads back independently. It compares actual statuses and text with those unchanged cases. A passed schema or exact string is mechanical evidence, not general semantic review.
5. changes.json and support.json carry lessons 10–15 forward: reject an automatic-publication scope change, name manual fallback and assign maintenance/retention responsibility. The script creates a real backup, restores into a distinct file and compares rows through fresh connections. It does not simply trust a supplied recovery=true flag.
6. The report, client-email.md and case-study.md carry lessons 16–18 into the same client identity. They distinguish local checks, illustrative projections and unperformed human acceptance. Do not replace untested fields with passed to make the handover look complete. The generated email is a local file; nothing is sent.

### engagement.py

```python
"""A complete fictional client delivery. Observations are produced, not prefilled passed flags."""
import argparse,csv,hashlib,json,shutil,sqlite3,time
from pathlib import Path

def fingerprint(path):return hashlib.sha256(path.read_bytes()).hexdigest()
def load(root,name):return json.loads((root/name).read_text())
def deliver(root,out):
 root=root.resolve();out.mkdir(parents=True,exist_ok=True)
 offer=load(root,'offer.json');scope=load(root,'scope.json');baseline=load(root,'baseline.json');data=load(root,'data-policy.json');support=load(root,'support.json');changes=load(root,'changes.json');tests=load(root,'acceptance.json')
 if offer['client']!=scope['client'] or support['client']!=scope['client']:raise ValueError('client_identity_disagreement')
 if set(offer['promises'])-set(scope['included']):raise ValueError('offer_exceeds_scope')
 if not set(scope['acceptance_ids'])<=set(t['id'] for t in tests):raise ValueError('acceptance_missing')
 if data['approved_fields']!=['id','source','text'] or data['retention_days']!=7:raise ValueError('data_policy')
 if any(c['decision']=='included' and c['request'] not in scope['included'] for c in changes):raise ValueError('unapproved_scope_change')
 source=list(csv.DictReader((root/'requests.csv').open(newline='')))
 if any(set(row)!=set(data['approved_fields']) for row in source):raise ValueError('unapproved_data_fields')
 database=out/'pilot.sqlite3'
 if database.exists():raise FileExistsError('Use a fresh output directory; retain prior evidence.')
 with sqlite3.connect(database) as db:
  db.execute('CREATE TABLE drafts(id TEXT PRIMARY KEY,source TEXT NOT NULL,text TEXT,status TEXT NOT NULL)')
  for row in source:
   # Extract the authorized source verbatim; missing evidence routes to review.
   status='draft_for_review' if row['source']=='policy-v1' and row['text'].strip() else 'needs_source'
   db.execute('INSERT INTO drafts VALUES(?,?,?,?)',(row['id'],row['source'],row['text'] if status=='draft_for_review' else None,status))
 with sqlite3.connect(database) as db:
  actual={r[0]:{'source':r[1],'text':r[2],'status':r[3]} for r in db.execute('SELECT * FROM drafts')}
 results=[]
 for test in tests:
  got=actual.get(test['request_id']);passed=got is not None and got['status']==test['status'] and got['text']==test['text']
  results.append({'id':test['id'],'passed':passed,'actual':got})
 # Fresh destination and connection establish an actual restore, not a copied boolean.
 backup=out/'backup.sqlite3';restored=out/'restored.sqlite3';start=time.perf_counter()
 with sqlite3.connect(database) as db,sqlite3.connect(backup) as target:db.backup(target)
 shutil.copy2(backup,restored)
 with sqlite3.connect(restored) as db:restored_rows=list(db.execute('SELECT * FROM drafts ORDER BY id'))
 with sqlite3.connect(database) as db:original_rows=list(db.execute('SELECT * FROM drafts ORDER BY id'))
 recovery=restored_rows==original_rows
 restore_ms=(time.perf_counter()-start)*1000
 # Economics are an illustrative business estimate, separate from measured script time.
 projected=baseline['weekly_items']*(baseline['old_minutes']-baseline['new_review_minutes'])-baseline['weekly_maintenance_minutes']
 ready=all(r['passed'] for r in results) and recovery and support['manual_fallback']=='retain input and use approved source manually'
 report={'client':scope['client'],'engagement':'fictional_simulation','mechanical_acceptance':results,'restore_readback':recovery,'restore_ms':round(restore_ms,3),'projected_minutes_saved':projected,'projection_measured':False,'semantic_client_acceptance':'not_performed','real_client_deployment':False,'scoped_local_handover':ready,'usage_cost_usd':None,'retention_days':data['retention_days'],'context_policy':'only approved source text; no cross-client reuse','input_versions':{p.name:fingerprint(p) for p in root.iterdir() if p.is_file()}}
 (out/'acceptance-report.json').write_text(json.dumps(report,indent=2))
 (out/'client-email.md').write_text(f"# Simulated handover to {scope['client']}\n\nThe local pilot produced {len(results)} mechanical checks and an isolated restore readback of {recovery}. Human factual acceptance and real client hosting remain untested. No automatic sending or publication is included. The time-saving projection ({projected} minutes/week) is illustrative, not observed ROI.\n")
 (out/'case-study.md').write_text('# Fictional case study\n\nA source-extractive handout pilot preserves missing evidence as needs_source. It writes reviewed drafts, not published content. Consult acceptance-report.json for measured local checks, unresolved factual acceptance and illustrative business assumptions. This is a training engagement and may not be marketed as a client result.\n')
 if not ready:raise ValueError('mechanical_acceptance_failed; inspect retained report')
 return report
if __name__=='__main__':
 parser=argparse.ArgumentParser();parser.add_argument('--inputs',type=Path,default=Path('client'));parser.add_argument('--output',type=Path,required=True);args=parser.parse_args();report=deliver(args.inputs,args.output);print(json.dumps({'scoped_local_handover':report['scoped_local_handover'],'client_acceptance':report['semantic_client_acceptance']}))
```
